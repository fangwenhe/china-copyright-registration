# China Copyright Registration 🇨🇳📝

> A practical, bilingual guide for **foreign creators, designers, developers and companies** registering copyright in China — works (作品著作权) and software (软件著作权/软著).

**Why register in China?** China's copyright law protects works *automatically* at creation, but a **voluntary registration certificate (自愿登记证书)** is the strongest, cheapest proof of ownership you can hold — it accelerates platform takedowns (Tmall, Taobao, Douyin, TikTok Shop), supports licensing deals, and gives you a clear evidence chain in court or before Chinese authorities.

## Why register in China

1. **Proof of ownership** — the certificate names the author/owner and the creation date; it is the go-to evidence in complaints, disputes and litigation.
2. **Platform takedowns** — Tmall/Taobao, Douyin, TikTok Shop, WeChat and JD IP-complaint systems accept copyright certificates to remove infringing listings/videos fast.
3. **Licensing & deals** — Chinese licensees, distributors and platforms routinely ask for a certificate before signing.
4. **Anti-squatting** — in disputes, an early certificate beats "I posted it on social media first" arguments in practice.
5. **Cheap** — software copyright registration is **free** (official fee ¥0 in most cases); works registration is a small official fee.

> ⚠️ **Social media posts are weak evidence.** A Douyin/Xiaohongshu/Twitter timestamp shows *when a public post existed*, but it does not formally prove authorship or content completeness, and it is easy to challenge (deleted posts, edited versions, third-party tools). A copyright registration certificate is the professional, court-friendly form of proof. Register first, post later.

## Two registration tracks

| Track | What it covers | Official body | Official fee | Typical timeline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Works copyright** (作品著作权) | Literary, art/design, music, video, photography, audiobooks, brands' visual assets | China Copyright Protection Center (CCPC, 中国版权保护中心) | ~¥0–300/件 (category-dependent) | 30–60 working days |
| **Software copyright** (软件著作权/软著) | Source code & documentation of a computer program | CCPC | **Free** (¥0) in most cases | 30–60 working days |

## Works registration: step by step

1. **Prepare** — title, author(s), owner, creation date & place, completion statement, samples (images/audio/video/PDF), and the work itself.
2. **File online** — CCPC online system (https://www.ccopyright.com.cn) with a Chinese ID/passport; foreign applicants file via the international route or a registered agency.
3. **Wait for examination** — CCPC reviews formalities; corrections may be requested.
4. **Receive certificate** — 登记证书 (Registration Certificate) with registration number.

## Software copyright (软著): step by step

1. **Check eligibility** — the software must be an original computer program (code + documentation).
2. **Prepare** — source code (first & last 30 pages or full), user manual/design documentation, ownership statement.
3. **File** — CCPC online application; submit code & docs in the required format (usually PDF).
4. **Examination & certificate** — ~30–60 working days; certificate names the software and owner.

> **Note for foreign software companies**: you can register software copyright in China as the author/owner of record. If you work with a Chinese distributor or OEM, registering in your own name (not the distributor's) protects your position.

## Required materials

- Application form (application via CCPC system generates it)
- Identity proof: passport / business registration (English documents usually need a Chinese translation)
- Work samples (for works) or source code + documentation (for software)
- Ownership statement / authorization letter if filing on behalf of the author
- Optional: prior certificates, published versions, contracts (for derivative works)

## FAQ

**Q: Do I own copyright in China if I registered it in the US/EU?**
A: China is a Berne Convention member — your work is protected automatically. But a **foreign registration is not the same as a Chinese certificate**: for local enforcement (platform complaints, customs, courts) a Chinese voluntary registration certificate is the most convenient evidence.

**Q: I posted my work on TikTok/Douyin/Xiaohongshu — isn't that enough proof?**
A: It proves a public post existed at a time, but it is **weak, challengeable evidence**: posts can be deleted or edited, and platforms' timestamps don't formally establish authorship or full content. A registration certificate is the accepted, professional proof. **Register first, post later.**

**Q: Can a foreigner register copyright in China?**
A: Yes — foreign individuals and companies can register works/software copyright in China. You can file via CCPC's international route or a registered agency that handles the translation and filing.

**Q: Does my app's copyright protect my trademark/logo too?**
A: No — copyright protects the work (code, images, text) while a **trademark** protects brand names/logos in commerce. Most brands need both: trademark for the brand, copyright for the assets. See also [Awesome China IP](https://github.com/fangwenhe/awesome-china-ip).

**Q: How do I enforce a certificate on Tmall/Douyin?**
A: Use the platform's IP complaint portal (品牌保护平台), upload the certificate + infringing link + evidence; platforms typically act within days for verified complaints.

## Enforcement & platform takedowns

1. **Platform complaint** — Tmall/Taobao, Douyin, TikTok Shop, WeChat, JD: use the brand-protection/IP portal, attach your certificate.
2. **Administrative** — complain to the local Copyright Administration (版权局) for takedowns of pirated content.
3. **Civil litigation** — Chinese IP courts accept the certificate as strong prima facie evidence.
4. **Customs** — record your copyright to intercept pirated imports/exports.
